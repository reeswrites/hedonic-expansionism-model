# Assumptions

Every assumption the model and the theory rest on, including those that are only implicit in the code. The companion to [theory.md](theory.md).

## Status key

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **NORMATIVE** | A value choice. Evidence cannot settle it; it has to be defended by argument and could reasonably be chosen differently. |
| **EVIDENCE** | Supported by cited empirical work. The tag after the citation follows the prior-art notes: *verified* (claim checked against an abstract or full text), *partly verified* (citation checked, some details not), *memory* (not checked; a lead only). Evidence usually supports the **direction** of an assumption, not its functional form or numbers. |
| **ARGUED** | Supported by argument only, with no direct evidence found. |
| **INVENTED** | A parameter value or functional form chosen without data, for convenience or illustration. |

Many rows carry two statuses, for example "EVIDENCE (direction) / INVENTED (form and values)." **Be clear about the base rate: nearly every numeric parameter in [`model/`](../model/) is INVENTED.** The exceptions are the acquired-taste curve (fitted to one music study) and the per-kind adaptation shares in the adaptation-ladder run E7 (set from the literature, at the level of rough bands). Proofs establish what follows *from* these assumptions; they say nothing about whether the assumptions hold.

"What changes if wrong" cites a sensitivity result where one exists. Where no sensitivity run exists, it says so; those are the gaps most worth closing.

## 1. Values and normative choices

| Assumption | Where used | Status | What changes if it's wrong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pleasure and suffering are the only intrinsic goods and bads (ethical hedonism). | Every value function, e.g. $V = \sum_t \delta^t(P_t - \lambda S_t)$ in [hx_p4.py](../model/hx_p4.py), [hx_pleasure_relief.py](../model/hx_pleasure_relief.py) | NORMATIVE | The whole frame. On a desire-satisfaction or objective-list theory, expansion could be neutral or matter for other reasons. |
| Value aggregates additively over time within a life. | All scripts | NORMATIVE | Peak-end or narrative-shape views of a life would weight episodes differently. Remembered value tracks peak and end, not duration (Alaybek et al. 2022, *verified*), so additivity is a claim about experienced, not remembered, value. |
| Suffering weighs more than pleasure, by a constant $\lambda$: 1.5 in [hx_p4.py](../model/hx_p4.py), [hx_relief_types*.py](../model/hx_relief_types.py), [hx_pleasure_relief.py](../model/hx_pleasure_relief.py), [hx_social_vs_solo.py](../model/hx_social_vs_solo.py), [hx_alone_vs_social.py](../model/hx_alone_vs_social.py), [hx_broad_social_vs_solo.py](../model/hx_broad_social_vs_solo.py); 2.0 in [hx_model.py](../model/hx_model.py), [hx_social.py](../model/hx_social.py), [hx_updates.py](../model/hx_updates.py); loss weight 2.0 in [hx_yield_reference.py](../model/hx_yield_reference.py), [hx_adaptation_ladder.py](../model/hx_adaptation_ladder.py). | P4, P4b, P4c, P6, S1, S2, social and reference-point scripts | NORMATIVE (the weight) / INVENTED (the values). Not calibrated: loss aversion of about 2 is a *decision* weight (Brown et al. 2024, *verified*), and in experience losses hurt less than forecast (Kermer et al. 2006, *verified*; Gal and Rucker 2018, *verified*). | **No script sweeps $\lambda$.** Known directions: the relief threshold $S^*$ falls as $\lambda$ rises ([P4](../proofs/P4_bottleneck.md)), so a lower $\lambda$ shifts effort toward expansion; the bundle threshold $b^*$ is linear in $\lambda$ ([P6](../proofs/P5_P6_grief_and_bundles.md)); the widening-circle condition $\eta/\kappa < G/(\lambda S)$ tightens as $\lambda$ rises ([S2](../proofs/S_social.md)); with reference points, the high-floor portfolio's advantage over high-peak exists only with loss weight above 1 ([results](../results/hx_yield_reference.txt), E1). |
| The suffering weight is constant, not intensity-dependent. | Same as above | INVENTED (form). Evidence suggests a convex weight: positivity offset at low intensity, steeper negative gradients at high (Rozin and Royzman 2001, *verified*). | A convex weight would strengthen the result that the relief share rises with distress ([P4](../proofs/P4_bottleneck.md)). Not run. |
| Suffering inputs are not already weighted by negativity bias (no double counting). | All scripts with $\lambda$ | ARGUED, implicitly; the units of $S$ are never specified. | If $S$ is felt intensity, $\lambda > 1$ counts negativity bias twice ([evolution_cognition.md §2](../prior-art/evolution_cognition.md)). |
| Quantity, not rank: no higher and lower pleasures. | Implicit in every model (a unit of liking is a unit of liking whatever its source) | NORMATIVE | With a Millian ranking, depth in "higher" domains would dominate breadth; the content constraints would partly become a ranking. |
| Universal: everyone's pleasure and suffering count equally (moral level). | Theory only | NORMATIVE | Distinct from the model's $\kappa$, which is how much others' pleasure *registers as one's own experience* (psychological). The model has no moral aggregation rule. |
| Group welfare is the sum of individuals' experienced value $U_i$, which includes vicarious terms. | [hx_social.py](../model/hx_social.py) S4, [hx_pair.py](../model/hx_pair.py) | NORMATIVE / implicit | Summing $U_i$ counts each pleasure once for its owner and again for everyone who feels *mudita* toward them, so higher $\kappa$ inflates "experienced value" mechanically. The S4 caveat already flags own-source welfare as the cleaner measure (3.05 vs 4.92 experienced, same setup). |
| Pure time discounting of one's own future, $\delta$ = 0.97–0.999 per period. | All dynamic scripts | NORMATIVE (that one discounts at all) / INVENTED (values) | Expansion pays only over long horizons ([P3](../proofs/P3_expansion_worth_it.md); in [hx_acquired_taste.py](../model/hx_acquired_taste.py) almost nothing was worth acquiring over 50 periods). A lower $\delta$ shortens the effective horizon and makes expansion fail more often. |
| Risk neutrality: choices are compared in expected value. | Assumptions C1 ([P3](../proofs/P3_expansion_worth_it.md)), G2 ([P5/P6](../proofs/P5_P6_grief_and_bundles.md)), Q1 ([S](../proofs/S_social.md)) | NORMATIVE / implicit | Risk aversion over outcomes would make breadth valuable even under linear grief, partly substituting for the convex-grief premise. |
| Freedom (floor, menu, exit) is a rule-level constraint justified by pleasure and suffering, not a separate value. | Theory only; not modeled | NORMATIVE / ARGUED | If freedom is an independent value, some hedonically better arrangements become impermissible for a different reason. |
| Permission, not obligation. | Theory; supported by [P3](../proofs/P3_expansion_worth_it.md) failing for many parameter values | NORMATIVE | If expansion were a duty (perfectionism), the guardrails against pressure would weaken. |
| Content matters only through effects (renewal, skill slope, rivalry, what desire it trains). | [§5 of theory.md](theory.md#5-content-constraints) | ARGUED | A realist view (Kagan 2009) would let content matter independently of effects. |
| Causing suffering is worse than allowing it, with allowing discounted by cost and distance. | Theory only | NORMATIVE (provisional) | Not modeled; affects how indirect harms count against a liking. |

## 2. Psychology of liking and wanting

| Assumption | Where used | Status | What changes if it's wrong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Liking and wanting are separable systems. | [P7](../proofs/P7_wanting.md), [hx_pleasure_relief.py](../model/hx_pleasure_relief.py), [hx_adaptation_ladder.py](../model/hx_adaptation_ladder.py) E5 | EVIDENCE: Berridge and Robinson 2016 (*verified*); Ihssen and Wadsley 2021 on feeds (*verified*) | Sub-claim 4.1 collapses; the liking-not-wanting constraint would lose its mechanism. |
| Liking of a source is one scalar $\ell \ge 0$, combined multiplicatively: pleasure $= g(S)\,\ell\,h\,u$. | [hx_model.py](../model/hx_model.py), [P1](../proofs/P1_no_expansion.md), [P2](../proofs/P2_breadth_bounded.md), [hx_p4.py](../model/hx_p4.py) | INVENTED (form) | Interactions between numbing, freshness and liking (for example, stress numbing fresh sources more) are excluded. Uncertainty and surprise jointly shape pleasure (Cheung et al. 2019, *verified*), which one freshness scalar cannot represent. |
| Pleasure is linear in time share $u$ within a period. | [P1](../proofs/P1_no_expansion.md), [P2](../proofs/P2_breadth_bounded.md) | INVENTED | With concave returns to time within a period, breadth would be worth more than P2 shows. |
| Liking strength is unbounded through depth or discovery. | Open-endedness claim of the thesis | ARGUED; an empirical premise | If bounded, reach is bounded too and the thesis reduces to "breadth for resilience" ([P2](../proofs/P2_breadth_bounded.md)). |
| Pleasure in a moment saturates at a ceiling, $v = C\tanh(\mathrm{pos}/C) - (1-e)\,C\tanh(\mathrm{neg}/C)$, $C = 3$. | [hx_model_v1.py](../model/hx_model_v1.py) and every v1/v2/arousal script | ARGUED (a ceiling exists) / INVENTED (tanh form, $C$) | The refinement lemma needs only that value increases in liked mass ([lemma, F3](../proofs/refinement_lemma.md)). "Time near ceiling" (threshold 60% of $C$) depends directly on $C$ and the form. |
| Wanting is a cached prediction plus additive sensitization, $w = v + s$. | [hx_p7.py](../model/hx_p7.py) | EVIDENCE (form): Zhang et al. 2009 allow additive or multiplicative state modulation / INVENTED ($\eta_v = 0.5$, $\sigma$, $d$) | A multiplicative form would make lock-in depend on cached value, not only on sensitization. The lock-in condition ([P7b](../proofs/P7_wanting.md)) would change. |
| The wanting–liking gap is the same for all sources. | [hx_p7.py](../model/hx_p7.py), [hx_pleasure_relief.py](../model/hx_pleasure_relief.py) | INVENTED. Evidence suggests it is larger for evolutionarily novel, concentrated sources (Nesse and Berridge 1997, *verified*). | Lock-in risk would concentrate in a few source types; better alternatives lower it (Lenoir et al. 2007; Venniro et al. 2018, *verified* summaries). Not modeled. |
| Urge surfing lowers how often the urge steers ($\pi$), not the learned value. | [hx_p7.py](../model/hx_p7.py) | ARGUED; consistent with Bowen and Marlatt 2009 (urges unchanged, smoking reduced); an app trial found no abstinence effect (Garrison et al., via OpenAlex) | Under Brewer's account, mindful attention lowers cached value instead. Different mechanism, possibly similar outcome; not compared ([P7 limits](../proofs/P7_wanting.md)). |
| Grabbing (wanting-driven use) depletes liking, narrows attention, builds no liking, and raises the urge. | [hx_pleasure_relief.py](../model/hx_pleasure_relief.py) | ARGUED / INVENTED (rates). Galak et al. 2013 (*verified*) support "self-paced too fast, less enjoyment." | Ablation ([hx_pleasure_relief_phased.py](../model/hx_pleasure_relief_phased.py)): ignoring freshness alone takes value from 29 to −103; urge growth turns it compulsive (−180). Without urge growth, grabbing is bad but not compulsive. |
| Liking is fractal: an activity at resolution $r$ offers $N(r) = b\,r^D$ features; a coarse feature is the average of its parts. | [hx_model_v1.py](../model/hx_model_v1.py), [refinement_lemma.md](../proofs/refinement_lemma.md) | INVENTED. No formal precedent; Redden 2008 (*not read*) on sub-categorization reducing satiation is the nearest analogue. Naive version contradicted by Rocklage et al. 2021 (*verified*). | If coarse liking is not an average (for example, a whole is liked more than its parts), the lemma's part (a) does not follow. |
| Domain "fractal dimensions" $D$: chores 0.5, commute 0.6, meals 1.5, friends 1.6, music 1.8; shares of liked parts 0.45–0.80. | [hx_model_v1.py](../model/hx_model_v1.py) | INVENTED | Drives the dull-middle results; raising $D$ for chores (0.5 → 1.5) was the second-strongest lever ([theory §4.5](theory.md#45-breadth-and-depth)). |
| Finest-part likings drawn from exponential distributions (positive Exp(1), negative Exp(0.8)). | [hx_model_v1.py](../model/hx_model_v1.py) | INVENTED | Heavier aversive tails would make capture, and so equanimity, matter more. |
| Categorizing dulls pleasant **and** aversive parts by $(1 - k(1-c))$. | [hx_v2_symmetric.py](../model/hx_v2_symmetric.py) | EVIDENCE, weak: labeling dampens negative affect (Lieberman et al. 2007, *partly verified*); for positive affect thin and partly contrary (Vlasenko et al. 2020 poster, *verified*, not peer reviewed). Explanation dampens both (Wilson and Gilbert 2008, *verified*). | The pleasant-only version ([hx_model_v2.py](../model/hx_model_v2.py)) fails to reproduce "expertise numbs": experts got more pained, not more numb. The v2 conclusion (equanimity first, then concept release) rests on this choice. |
| Judging grades each part against the best in its domain. | [hx_model_v2.py](../model/hx_model_v2.py) | INVENTED | Failed to reproduce Stuppy and van den Bergh 2022 polarization under any principled rule; breaks refinement-lemma part (a) for many attention slots ([lemma, notes on v2](../proofs/refinement_lemma.md)). Probably wrong in detail. |
| Expertise raises clarity and categorizing load together. | [hx_model_v2.py](../model/hx_model_v2.py) | EVIDENCE: Rocklage et al. 2021 (*verified*) | If they can be decoupled by training, the "numbing" cost of expertise is avoidable. |
| Benign masochism: some initially aversive experiences become liked once the alarm is read as false; nausea and visceral pain never do. | Theory limits; [hx_yield_reference.py](../model/hx_yield_reference.py) threat sources | EVIDENCE: Rozin et al. 2013 (*verified*) | Defines the boundary of what expansion can reach. |
| New associations are added on top of old ones; core preferences cannot be erased. | Theory ("add, don't erase") | ARGUED | If erasure were possible, chosen contraction would be a stronger tool, and the coercion risk larger. |
| Pleasure can be both felt quality and attitude (hybrid account). | Theory | ARGUED (Feldman 2004 for the attitudinal half) | A purely felt-quality account struggles with heterogeneity; a purely attitudinal one with the liking/wanting split. |

## 3. Adaptation

| Assumption | Where used | Status | What changes if it's wrong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Freshness decays with use and recovers with rest: $h' = h - \alpha u h + \rho(1-h)(1-u)$, $\alpha = 0.6$, $\rho = 0.3$ (0.5 / 0.15–0.3 elsewhere). | [hx_model.py](../model/hx_model.py), [P1](../proofs/P1_no_expansion.md), [P2](../proofs/P2_breadth_bounded.md), most scripts | EVIDENCE (direction): Galak et al. 2013, Nelson et al. 2009 / INVENTED (linear form, values) | $\kappa = \alpha/\rho$ sets how fast breadth's returns diminish ([P2](../proofs/P2_breadth_bounded.md)). Recovery is partly perceptual (Galak, Redden and Kruger 2009, *verified*), which a clock cannot capture. |
| Each source has its own freshness, with no spillover between similar sources. | [hx_model.py](../model/hx_model.py), v1, P7 | INVENTED / implicit. Satiety spills within a sensory profile (Myers Ernst and Epstein 2002, *verified*). | The adaptation ladder adds 50% spillover within a profile (INVENTED): spreading four items over four profiles beat one shared profile by 61% ([results](../results/hx_adaptation_ladder.txt), E3). Breadth across dissimilar sources is worth more than P2 shows. |
| Slow novelty recovers only by forgetting, at rate $\varepsilon \ll \rho$. | [P1 slow recovery](../proofs/P1_no_expansion.md); [hx_acquired_taste.py](../model/hx_acquired_taste.py) ($\varepsilon = 0.005$) | ARGUED / INVENTED ($\varepsilon$). No simulation of the P1 version. | If $\varepsilon = 0$ (permanent loss), the old depleting type returns and pleasure from those sources goes to zero. |
| Source types: renewing, depleting, skill-slope with cap $c$. | [hx_model.py](../model/hx_model.py), [P1](../proofs/P1_no_expansion.md) | EVIDENCE (framing): Stigler and Becker 1977 beneficial/harmful addiction / INVENTED ($\beta$, $\gamma$, $c$) | Which type a real pleasure is decides the P1 regime; that is the empirical content of the renewal and skill-slope constraints. |
| A moving reference point tracks a share $\phi$ of recent yield, at rate 0.03. | [hx_yield_reference.py](../model/hx_yield_reference.py), [hx_adaptation_ladder.py](../model/hx_adaptation_ladder.py) | EVIDENCE (existence): Rayo and Becker 2007, Robson 2001 (*verified*) / INVENTED (rate, form) | **The most consequential adaptation assumption.** At $\phi = 1$ expansion's gain vanishes or turns slightly negative ([results](../results/hx_yield_reference.txt), E2). |
| Adaptation is partial and differs by kind of good: $\phi$ = 0.95 material, 0.5 sensory, 0.3 social and skill, 0.2 sensitizing, 0 relief. | [hx_adaptation_ladder.py](../model/hx_adaptation_ladder.py) | EVIDENCE, rough bands: Sheldon and Lyubomirsky 2006, Di Tella et al. 2010, Lucas 2007, Carter and Gilovich 2010 ([adaptation_by_domain.md](../prior-art/adaptation_by_domain.md); mostly *verified* abstracts) / INVENTED (exact values) | E6: if skill and social sources adapt like purchases ($\phi \to 1$), their late gain turns negative (skill +0.626 → −0.146). The "activities, not acquisitions" claim depends on this. |
| Relief is exempt from the reference point. | [hx_yield_reference.py](../model/hx_yield_reference.py), [hx_adaptation_ladder.py](../model/hx_adaptation_ladder.py) | INVENTED / ARGUED (relief is measured against the deficit state) | "Relief is the floor that survives adaptation" (E3) is built in by this assumption, not found. |
| Pain and pleasure adapt at the same rate. | Implicit in every script with one freshness or reference rule | INVENTED / implicit. Evidence says pain adapts less (Perez-Truglia 2012, *verified* abstract; disability, noise and commuting barely adapt). | Reducing suffering would be a more durable gain than the model shows, strengthening the relief-weighting results. Not run. |
| No aspiration creep in the base models (v0–v2, P-series). | [hx_model.py](../model/hx_model.py), v1, v2, P1–P7 | INVENTED / implicit. Aspiration creep is one of two adaptation paths (Sheldon and Lyubomirsky 2012, *partly verified*). | Those results overstate long-run gains; the reference-point scripts partly correct this. |
| Adaptation runs through attention moving away. | Motivates per-kind $\phi$; not derived in the model | EVIDENCE: Kahneman and Thaler 2006 (*verified*) | If adaptation is not attention-driven, the link between equanimity/attention and durability ([theory §4.6](theory.md#46-capacity-and-content)) weakens. |
| Exposure mode multiplies satiety: focused ×1.5, background ×0.6, forced ×1.5, interrupted ×0.6; using past the plateau delays recovery 6 periods. | [hx_adaptation_ladder.py](../model/hx_adaptation_ladder.py) E2 | EVIDENCE (direction): Nelson et al. 2009, Rankin et al. 2009 (*partly verified*) / INVENTED (all numbers) | E2's ranking of modes (interrupted, chosen use best; forced costs about 4%) is illustrative only. |
| "Sensitizing" sources whose liking rises with regular use. | [hx_adaptation_ladder.py](../model/hx_adaptation_ladder.py) | ARGUED / weak EVIDENCE. Wanting sensitizes with deprivation (von Mohr et al. 2021, *verified*); no study found of *liking* sensitizing for natural pleasures. | Sensitizing sources were among the best expansions (late gain +0.4 to +0.5). If the type does not exist, that result goes. |
| Motivation (wanting) habituates faster than liking. | [hx_adaptation_ladder.py](../model/hx_adaptation_ladder.py) | Weak EVIDENCE: lab food studies show both drop together; only confounded field-ration data suggest otherwise ([adaptation_by_domain.md §5](../prior-art/adaptation_by_domain.md)) | E5's wanting-policy results depend on it. |

## 4. Suffering and relief

| Assumption | Where used | Status | What changes if it's wrong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Suffering is a single scalar $S \ge 0$. | All suffering models: [hx_p4.py](../model/hx_p4.py), [hx_relief_types*.py](../model/hx_relief_types.py), [hx_pleasure_relief.py](../model/hx_pleasure_relief.py), social scripts | INVENTED / implicit. Evidence says types differ: threat but not negative feedback blunted reward learning (Bogdan and Pizzagalli 2006, *verified*); social and physical pain have separate neural representations (Woo et al. 2014, *verified*). | Different kinds would have different numbing $g$, learning $p$ and relief rates, so the threshold would be typed ([P4 limits](../proofs/P4_bottleneck.md)). Shared social relief and solitude relief may not be interchangeable with physical relief. Not modeled. |
| Suffering is bounded below by zero, so relief tops out. | Thesis (baseline bounded); $\varphi(0) = 0$ in [P4](../proofs/P4_bottleneck.md) | ARGUED | This is what makes baseline bounded and reach "where the open-ended good lives." |
| Suffering numbs liking: $g(S)$ decreasing, $g(0) = 1$; exponential with $k = 0.25$–0.3 (linear and logistic variants in [hx_p4.py](../model/hx_p4.py)). | P4, P4b, P4c, social scripts | EVIDENCE (direction): acute threat roughly halves reward responsiveness (Bogdan and Pizzagalli 2006); stress collapses affective discrimination (Reich and Zautra 2002) / INVENTED (form, $k$) | The threshold's *existence* needs only that relief has less to do as $S \to 0$ ([P4 remark](../proofs/P4_bottleneck.md)). The prediction that relief raises positive feeling most in the most distressed needs concave $g$; with exponential $g$ the gain peaks at moderate distress ([P4 Prop. 2](../proofs/P4_bottleneck.md)). |
| Distress lowers the success of expansion: $p(S)$ decreasing. | P3, P4, acquired taste | EVIDENCE (direction): Lenow et al. 2017 (stress reduces exploration), Feeney and Thrush 2010 (secure base) / INVENTED (form) | With $p$ constant the threshold still exists ([P4 remark](../proofs/P4_bottleneck.md)), so this mainly lowers it. |
| Effort is one fungible unit per period, split between relief and expansion. | [hx_p4.py](../model/hx_p4.py), [hx_relief_types*.py](../model/hx_relief_types.py), [hx_pleasure_relief.py](../model/hx_pleasure_relief.py) | INVENTED / implicit | If relief and expansion draw on different resources (money and conditions versus attention and time), they are not rivals and the sequencing question partly dissolves; the "weight effort by what binds" claim would apply only where they do compete. |
| Constant returns to effort within each action (giving an all-or-nothing switch), or concave returns $h(x) = x^\beta$ (giving a gradient). | [P4 Theorems 1 and 3](../proofs/P4_bottleneck.md); [hx_updates.py](../model/hx_updates.py) | INVENTED (both) | The gate-versus-gradient conclusion is entirely this assumption: linear gives a switch, any strictly concave $h$ gives an interior, rising relief share. Splitting beat switching by up to 28% at $\beta = 0.5$. |
| Relief is proportional to suffering, $\varphi(S) = \rho S$; clean relief removes 25%–30% per unit. | [hx_p4.py](../model/hx_p4.py), relief scripts | INVENTED | Any strictly increasing $\varphi$ with $\varphi(0) = 0$ gives the threshold ([P4, A3](../proofs/P4_bottleneck.md)). |
| Numbing relief is twice as fast as clean relief (50% vs 25%) but costs capacity $\mu$ per dose and adds a rebound. | [hx_relief_types.py](../model/hx_relief_types.py), [hx_relief_types2.py](../model/hx_relief_types2.py) | EVIDENCE (structure): Solomon 1980, Koob and Le Moal 2001, Baker et al. 2004, Hayes et al. 1996 / INVENTED (all numbers) | Sweep over $\mu$ and rebound: with $\mu = 0$ numbing all the way down is best (344 vs 254); at $\mu = 0.15$ one dose; at $\mu = 0.3$ never ([results](../results/hx_relief_types2.txt)). "Numbing is a bridge" holds only with a real capacity cost. |
| Rebound is constant, or grows with use and decays (opponent process). | [P4b](../proofs/P4b_numbing_loop.md); [hx_updates.py](../model/hx_updates.py) part 3 | EVIDENCE (opponent-process form): Solomon 1980 / INVENTED (values) | Closed-form loop condition $r \ge \theta(1-(1-\rho_n)(1-d))$; with opponent-process rebound, lock-in tightens and a minimum safe spacing exists only if one dose cannot re-trigger on its own. |
| Suffering fades on its own at rate $d$. | [hx_relief_types2.py](../model/hx_relief_types2.py), [P4b](../proofs/P4b_numbing_loop.md) | INVENTED | Habitual numbing is worse than doing nothing at $d = 0.1$ (−38 vs 184) but better at $d = 0.03$ (−41 vs −108) ([P4b](../proofs/P4b_numbing_loop.md)). The headline "worse than nothing" depends on suffering fading. |
| Pleasure relieves suffering in proportion to its quality: $\rho_p\,q\,S$, $\rho_p = 0.4$. | [hx_pleasure_relief.py](../model/hx_pleasure_relief.py), [P4c](../proofs/P4c_pleasure_relief_crossover.md) | EVIDENCE (direction): undoing effect, Fredrickson and Levenson 1998 / INVENTED (form, value) | The crossover $S_\times = \ln(\rho_p/\rho_c)/k \approx 1.57$ depends on $\rho_p/\rho_c$ and $k$; if $\rho_p \le \rho_c$, savoring never out-relieves clean relief. |
| Expansion can itself relieve (behavioral activation, $\psi$); stressors can arrive faster than relief clears them ($\sigma$). | [P4 Prop. 4](../proofs/P4_bottleneck.md), [hx_p4.py](../model/hx_p4.py) | EVIDENCE (BA efficacy): Dimidjian et al. 2006 via Kanter et al. 2012 / INVENTED ($\psi$, $\sigma$) | These are the stated exceptions; their size decides how often the base rule applies. |

## The assumptions that matter most

Ranked by how much a conclusion changes if the assumption fails:

1. **How complete adaptation is, by kind of good** (reference point $\phi$). Under full adaptation, expansion's value vanishes ([results/hx_yield_reference.txt](../results/hx_yield_reference.txt), E2; [results/hx_adaptation_ladder.txt](../results/hx_adaptation_ladder.txt), E6). This is the central empirical question for the theory.
2. **Grief shape.** Convex grief is required for breadth to buffer loss; concave grief reverses it ([P5/P6](../proofs/P5_P6_grief_and_bundles.md)).
3. **Size of shared relief.** It decides whether, and how much, a social life beats a solo one ([results/hx_social_vs_solo.txt](../results/hx_social_vs_solo.txt), T5).
4. **Concave returns to effort.** The gradient-not-gate conclusion is this assumption ([P4 Theorem 3](../proofs/P4_bottleneck.md)).
5. **Single scalar suffering and fungible effort.** Implicit throughout; both are probably false, and neither has been relaxed.
6. **The suffering weight $\lambda$.** Normative, and possibly double-counting negativity bias. Swept from 1 to 5 in [hx_assumption_checks.py](../model/hx_assumption_checks.py): the combined-model verdicts hold except the small edge of a few close people over a broad solo life, which is gone at $\lambda = 5$. The default-vs-expansionist gap grows with $\lambda$ (2.7 to 4.7) through numbing, while the gap without a numbing source barely moves (1.3 to 1.6).
7. **Liking on one scale across people.** Implicit in every social result.
8. **Equanimity as a free parameter.** Now also run as a costly skill that takes practice time and decays without it ([hx_assumption_checks.py](../model/hx_assumption_checks.py)): heavy early practice plus maintenance pays back in about 35–65 periods; practicing only when distressed never pays, because distress blocks learning; the order of the calm skills matters again (equanimity first under chronic distress, concept release first under ordinary distress); results depend strongly on the invented decay rate.
9. **Pain adapting at the same rate as pleasure.** Checked: making suffering fade more slowly raises relief's value as a floor by up to 45% and makes sensory expansion fade faster, but leaves the other verdicts and the overall gap unchanged.
